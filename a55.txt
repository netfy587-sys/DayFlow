<section class="intro" id="intro">
  <div class="wrap intro-grid">
    <div>
      <div class="section-head">
        <span class="tag">THE SPORT</span>
        <h2>Not one martial art — all of them</h2>
      </div>
      <p class="lede">MMA fighters are judged on their ability to strike standing up, control an opponent against the cage, and finish a fight on the ground — three completely different skill sets in one bout.</p>
      <p class="body-text">That range is what separates MMA from a single-discipline sport like boxing or judo. A fighter with excellent boxing but no takedown defence can be brought to the mat and neutralised; a champion wrestler with no submission skill can be caught in a choke the moment the fight hits the floor. Success depends on being dangerous everywhere, not just in one phase of the fight.</p>
      <p class="body-text">Bouts happen inside a fenced enclosure — commonly called "the cage" or, in some promotions, the Octagon — rather than a roped ring, partly because the fence lets fighters strike or work off it without the risk of falling through the ropes.</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <img src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1680022702604-292f21514497?auto=format&fit=crop&w=900&q=80" alt="Two fighters standing next to each other inside an MMA cage" style="width:100%; height:520px; object-fit:cover;">
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="disciplines" id="disciplines">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="tag" style="color:#e07a3f;">FOUNDATIONS</span>
      <h2>The arts that built the sport</h2>
      <p class="lede">Every top MMA fighter is really cross-training in several older combat sports, each contributing a different layer to the game.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="disc-grid">
      <div class="disc-card">
        <div class="d-name">Boxing</div>
        <p>Hand striking, head movement, and footwork — the foundation of stand-up offence and defence.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="disc-card">
        <div class="d-name">Muay Thai</div>
        <p>Adds elbows, knees, and clinch fighting, widening the striking game beyond just punches.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="disc-card">
        <div class="d-name">Wrestling</div>
        <p>Takedowns and the ability to control where a fight happens — standing, against the cage, or on the mat.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="disc-card">
        <div class="d-name">Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu</div>
        <p>Ground control and submissions — chokes and joint locks that can end a fight without a single strike.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="disc-card">
        <div class="d-name">Judo &amp; Sambo</div>
        <p>Throws and off-balancing from a standing clinch, often used to bring a fight to the ground on a fighter's own terms.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="history" id="history">
  <div class="wrap history-grid">
    <img src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1680022548963-1d8e630a272b?auto=format&fit=crop&w=900&q=80" alt="Two fighters standing on top of an MMA cage before a match">
    <div>
      <div class="section-head">
        <span class="tag">ORIGINS</span>
        <h2>From no-rules spectacle to regulated sport</h2>
      </div>
      <p>The modern sport traces back to 1993, when the UFC held its first event as a near-anything-goes tournament designed to answer a simple question: which fighting style wins in a real contest? Early events had almost no weight classes, rounds, or rule limits.</p>
      <p>Royce Gracie, a Brazilian jiu-jitsu practitioner, won that first tournament and several after it, repeatedly submitting larger opponents and proving that ground fighting was as decisive as striking power — a result that reshaped how fighters trained from then on.</p>
      <p>Facing bans in several U.S. states over safety concerns, the sport adopted the Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts in the early 2000s, introducing weight classes, timed rounds, banned strikes, and licensed referees, which is largely the rulebook still used today.</p>
      <p>Since then, MMA has grown into a globally televised sport with promotions on every continent, and it remains one of the few major sports where a fighter's path to the top can run through wrestling mats, boxing gyms, and jiu-jitsu academies all at once.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="structure" id="structure">
  <div class="wrap structure-layout">
    <div>
      <div class="section-head">
        <span class="tag">TIMING</span>
        <h2>How a bout is structured</h2>
      </div>
      <p class="body-text" style="margin-bottom:6px;">Non-title fights run three five-minute rounds; championship bouts run five. A one-minute rest separates each round, and a fight can be stopped at any point if it's no longer competitive.</p>
      <div class="round-facts">
        <div class="fact"><div class="f-num">5 min</div><div class="f-desc">Length of each round, with a one-minute break between them.</div></div>
        <div class="fact"><div class="f-num">10-9</div><div class="f-desc">The standard scorecard system, borrowed directly from boxing, used when a fight goes the distance.</div></div>
        <div class="fact"><div class="f-num">3</div><div class="f-desc">Judges score every fight independently; a majority decides the winner on the cards.</div></div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div>
      <div class="section-head">
        <span class="tag">OUTCOMES</span>
        <h2>How a fight actually ends</h2>
      </div>
      <div class="win-list">
        <div class="win-row">
          <div class="w-name">Knockout</div>
          <div class="w-desc">A fighter is struck unconscious or unable to intelligently defend themselves.</div>
        </div>
        <div class="win-row">
          <div class="w-name">Submission</div>
          <div class="w-desc">A fighter taps out or verbally concedes rather than risk a choke or joint injury.</div>

        <div class="win-row">
          <div class="w-name">TKO / stoppage</div>

<section class="gear" id="gear">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="tag" style="color:#e07a3f;">EQUIPMENT</span>
      <h2>Gear built for both hands and grips</h2>
      <p class="lede">MMA gloves look nothing like boxing gloves, because fighters need to strike and grab in the same fight.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="gear-layout">
      <img src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1615117972428-28de67cda58e?auto=format&fit=crop&w=900&q=80" alt="A fighter wearing open-fingered MMA gloves">
      <dl class="gear-list">
        <dt>Open-finger gloves</dt>
        <dd>Padded at the knuckle but open at the fingers, typically 4–6oz, light enough to grip and grapple while still protecting the hand when striking.</dd>
        <dt>Mouthguard</dt>
        <dd>Required at every level, protecting the teeth and jaw from strikes at any range, standing or on the ground.</dd>
        <dt>Groin protection &amp; shin guards</dt>
        <dd>Worn under fight shorts; shin guards are standard in training but not used in professional competition.</dd>
        <dt>Rash guard or gi (training only)</dt>
        <dd>Used in jiu-jitsu and wrestling sessions to allow grips and drilling without skin-on-skin friction injuries.</dd>
        <dt>The cage</dt>
        <dd>A fenced, octagonal or hexagonal enclosure that keeps fighters in play against the perimeter rather than through open ropes.</dd>
      </dl>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
